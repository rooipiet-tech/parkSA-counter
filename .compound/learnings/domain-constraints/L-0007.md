statement: Temporal integrity for an offline, cross-timezone data app has two independent silent-corruption traps, both of which pass UTC-written tests. (1) Timezone binning: do all hour-of-day / day-of-week / calendar-date bucketing through ONE canonical conversion to the app's local zone; naive UTC binning shifts every hourly figure by the offset and moves late-evening events to the wrong calendar day. (2) Event-time vs processing-time: aggregate by the device timestamp captured at the event, not server received_at (which for offline-synced records is sync-arrival time), and write the 'was-offline/queued' flag on the CLIENT at enqueue — never infer it later from timestamp gaps.
evidence: DC-05/DC-06 HARD constraints; single verified SAST conversion point confirmed in sast.ts/aggregate.ts. Binning offline-synced events by server ts is silent corruption of the demand curve, and UTC-written tests stay green under wrong binning.
catch_next_time: Require a single shared tz-conversion choke point (function or SQL AT TIME ZONE) plus a mandatory boundary test at a cross-hour, cross-midnight local time; assert offline records aggregate by client device_ts with a client-written offline flag surfaced in export.
