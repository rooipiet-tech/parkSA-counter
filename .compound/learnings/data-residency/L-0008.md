statement: When the backend platform has no region in the data subjects jurisdiction (e.g. Supabase has no South African region; nearest is EU), the cheapest compliant design under POPIA/GDPR-style residency law is to store NO personal information at all, making the cross-border-transfer question moot rather than justifying an adequacy basis. Concretely: pseudonymous actor codes (validated, discouraging real names) instead of identities, and a hard ban on identifying captures (plates, photos, faces, precise GPS). Residual risk: a permissive pseudonym regex still admits a real first name, so enforcement rests on operator discipline — consider requiring a digit/prefix or a denylist.
evidence: DC-01/02/03 HARD; data-residency lens signed off with DR-1/DR-2 minor residuals ('Johan' passes the observer-code regex; location_label unbounded free text syncing to eu-west-1). Region fixed at project creation (eu-west-1), a one-shot decision.
catch_next_time: For any offshore-region backend, make the spec assert data minimization (no personal info stored) as the primary compliance path, validate all free-text fields that sync offshore against an identity-leak regex/length cap, and record the chosen region + rationale in a data-residency learning at project creation.
